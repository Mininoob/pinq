Pull a thought off your screen.

Pinq is a pad of sticky notes docked to the edge of your desktop. Pull one off, jot it down, throw it away. It lands in a library that is searchable, linked, and yours.

On every desk you own.

No account
Nothing to sign up for and no server of ours in the middle. Install it and pull a note.
Encrypted end to end
Devices pair with a code and a safety number, then sync directly. A relay, if you want one, only ever sees sealed envelopes.
A 24-word recovery phrase
The phrase is the key to your space. It restores a lost machine and unlocks your encrypted backups.
Smart, but on your device
Optional tag, title, and related-note suggestions come from a small model that runs locally. Your words stay put.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Pinq is MIT-licensed open source. There is no paid tier, no account, and no tracking.

Where are my notes stored?

On your computer, in Pinq's app data folder: a local database plus your attachments. When you pair devices, notes sync directly between them.

Do I need a server to sync?

No. Devices on the same network find each other and sync directly. For syncing across networks you can run the relay built into Pinq, and it only ever holds encrypted envelopes.

What happens if I lose a computer?

Your other paired devices still have everything. If every device is gone, the 24-word recovery phrase plus an encrypted backup brings your notes back.

Linux? Phones?

Windows and macOS are the supported builds today. Linux is untested; you can build from source. There is no mobile app yet.
