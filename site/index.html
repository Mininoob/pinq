Pull a thought off your screen.

Pinq is a pad of sticky notes that docks to the edge of your desktop. Pull a note off, write on it, and throw it away when you're done. It goes into a library you can search and link, stored on your own computer.

Lines break where you press Enter, and only there.

Write a line as long as the thought needs. The note gets wider instead of pushing your words onto the next line, and it opens at that width on your other computers too.

A sticky can also be a task.

Mark a note todo, doing, or done, and give it a due date that turns red if it slips. The progress bar fills as you tick boxes, and a reminder puts the note back on your screen when it's time.

Drag files onto it.

Drop an image or a file onto a note. Pictures show up in the note, and other files get a small chip with a download button. Both sync to your other computers with the note.

Type two brackets to link one note to another.

Type [[ and Pinq suggests the note you mean. If you rename a note, the links to it update. Each note lists the notes that link back to it, and the library can show all of them as a map.

Throwing a note away doesn't delete it.

Fling a note and it goes into your library, with an undo button in case you didn't mean it. Toss it gently at the edge of the screen instead and it folds into a thin tab. Hover to peek at it, or pull it back out.

Your notes on all your computers.

Pair your computers once, and notes sync straight between them, encrypted end to end. If the same note is open on both, you can watch the typing appear on the other one. If you edit it on both while offline, Pinq merges the two versions instead of throwing one away.

Notes come in seven colours, each can have its own paper texture, and the link map is built from your [[links]].

Your notes live on your computers, not ours.

No account
There's nothing to sign up for, and no Pinq server sits between your devices.
Encrypted end to end
You pair devices with a code and check a safety number, then they sync directly. If you use a relay, it only stores encrypted data it can't read.
A 24-word recovery phrase
The phrase is the key to your notes. Use it to set up a replacement computer or open an encrypted backup.
Suggestions that run on your computer
Pinq can suggest tags, titles, and related notes. The small model behind them runs locally, so your text never goes to a server. It's off until you turn it on.

Free and open source, under the MIT license.

Pinq is built with Rust and Tauri, with a TypeScript interface on top. You can read the code, fork it, build it yourself, or send a pull request.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Pinq is open source under the MIT license. There's no paid version, no account, and no tracking.

Where are my notes stored?

On your computer, in Pinq's app data folder: a local database plus your attachments. When you pair devices, notes sync directly between them.

Do I need a server to sync?

No. Devices on the same network find each other and sync directly. To sync across different networks, you can turn on the relay that's built into Pinq. It only ever holds encrypted data.

What happens if I lose a computer?

Your other paired devices still have everything. If every device is gone, your 24-word recovery phrase and an encrypted backup will bring your notes back.

Linux? Phones?

Windows and macOS are the supported builds right now. Linux hasn't been tested, but you can build it from source. There's no mobile app yet.
